Akaxia

I am a theoretical astrophysicist studying astrophysical probes of dark matter. In particular, I design, run, and use cosmological galaxy formation simulations and analytic methods to constrain dark matter models and derive astrophysical signatures. I am currently interested in how dark matter self-interactions can induce core-collapse in dark matter halos and alter galaxy and black hole formation and growth. I received my Ph.D. in physics from the University of Washington in 2023, where I was a National Science Foundation (NSF) Graduate Research Fellow. In 2019, I held an NSF GROW Fellowship at the Paris Institute of Astrophysics. As of the fall of 2023, I am a joint Flatiron Research Fellow at the Center for Computational Astrophysics and a Future Faculty in the Physical Sciences Postdoctoral Fellow in the Princeton University Department of Physics.

The SIDM Suite for Investigating Dwarf Morphologies:

If dark matter self-interacts like particles in the standard model, instead of being collisionless cold dark matter, dwarf scale properties can be significantly altered. In the introductory paper on The Self-interacting Dark Matter (SIDM) Suite for Investigating Dwarf Morphologies (SIDM-SIDM) zoom-ins, I showed that dark matter and stellar half-light radius co-evolve in irregular galaxies; if a disk forms, they co-evolve until the disk-forming merger and then decouple. Throughout the simulated sample, the co-evolution happens universally in all three dark matter cosmologies considered, but their unique evolutions alter the final galaxy sizes. By demonstrating that the underlying dark matter microphysics directly shapes galaxy sizes and morphologies, our work provides a bridge between dark matter physics and observed galaxy properties that can be critically tested by upcoming wide-field surveys like Rubin Observatory’s Legacy Survey of Space and Time.

Dark plasma

A simple way to make dark matter self-interact is to assume it behaves like electromagnetism (EM). In recent work I used analytic methods to show that the relative streaming of dark matter (such as in galaxy mergers) can cause initially small magnetic perturbations to become unstable, grow exponentially on timescales smaller than a Hubble time, and strongly couple dark matter to itself. I am now working to explore these plasma instabilities in simulations of merging dark matter halos which cause enhanced dark matter stripping.

Dark matter and black holes

The role of BHs on dwarf scales is still not well understood, yet intermediate-mass BHs (IMBHs) are expected to inhabit these systems. SIDM has been shown to significantly delay SMBH formation and growth in massive galaxies relative to CDM. This means upcoming observations from LISA may help probe the nature of dark matter through IMBH mergering.

Marvelous Massive Dwarf studies

Recent work with the Marvelous Massive Dwarf simulations have been conducted on dwarf disk formation, cyclic star formation in dipole disks, the Baryonic Tully-Fisher Relationship, the dispersion versus rotational support, and the properites of their CGM.
